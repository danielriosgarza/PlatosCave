# Parallax — review decisions

28 September 2026. The [product specification](product-spec.md) and [wireframe](wireframe.html) are the current design. This record resolves the [29 original review comments](reviews/fable-2026-09-28.md) and Fable’s counterproposal against the user’s brief and [layout reference](idea_draft.png). The separate counterproposal HTML was removed after unification; `wireframe.html` is the sole HTML wireframe.

Fable’s strongest contribution is making the stated workflows visible: a course-specific Resume action, a learning objective beneath the topic title, passage-aware notes, a complete exercise sequence, and traversal between students. Its visual language already fits the agreed white/grey/charcoal direction. The merge therefore uses its cleaner structure, with corrections where a demonstration would otherwise suggest the wrong product behaviour.

The merge also separates instructor draft preview from the release adopted by Class A. Editing a title or publishing a release cannot silently change student material. Unsent discussion text retains its selected audience, and private reading notes do not share a storage slot with slide notes.

The wireframe remains a design artifact. It does not authenticate, execute Python/R, connect to Shiny, persist annotations, or submit grades. The specification’s permission checks, immutable releases, secure execution, recovery, privacy, and submission rules remain intact. Prototype omissions are listed in specification §15 rather than silently removed from the product scope.

Verification covered nine fixture workflows: repeated slide-key navigation, separate slide annotations, full-screen/focus exit, note-save and audience state, three-step exercise completion, honest Shiny status, code-output staleness and receipt, filtered student grading, and draft-preview isolation. All five material tabs were checked at 1440, 1024, 768, 390, and 320 px without page overflow or JavaScript errors. The 1024 px embedded preview passed its Full screen fallback and Escape checks. These checks validate the demonstration, not the production acceptance scenarios. The finish reviewer scored both requested visual corrections resolved and returned **ship** for that correction pass.
